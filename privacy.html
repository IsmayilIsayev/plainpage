Plainpage privacy policy

Last updated: 17 November 2026

Plainpage Reader ("Plainpage") is a browser extension that shows web articles in a clean reading view. This policy explains what data it handles. The short version: Plainpage does not collect your data. The only thing that ever leaves your browser is a licence check, and only if you bought Plainpage Pro.

What Plainpage does not do

  • It does not collect, transmit or sell your browsing history, the pages you read, or their content.
  • It has no analytics, no telemetry, no advertising, no tracking and no user accounts.
  • It does not run on any page until you ask it to. If you turn on "Open the reader automatically" for a site, it runs on that site only, after you approve Chrome's permission prompt for it.

What is stored on your device

The following stays in your browser's extension storage:

  • Reading preferences (theme, font, size, width and similar). These are kept in Chrome's synced storage, so if you use Chrome sync, Google copies them between your own devices under Google's privacy policy.
  • Per-site preferences and the list of sites where the reader opens automatically, if you use those features.
  • Highlights and notes you create, together with the address and title of the page they belong to. These are kept only on this device.
  • Your licence key and its status, if you bought Plainpage Pro.

We cannot see any of this.

The one thing that is sent over the network

Plainpage Pro is sold by Polar (polar.sh), which acts as merchant of record and payment processor. If you enter a Plainpage Pro licence key, the extension sends a licence check to Polar's licence service at api.polar.sh. This happens when you activate or deactivate the key and at most once every seven days afterwards (with up to two quick retries if the connection fails). Each request contains only:

  • your licence key;
  • Plainpage's organization id at Polar (the same for every user; it identifies the seller, not you);
  • the activation id Polar created for this browser when you activated the key;
  • when activating, a device label naming your browser and operating system, for example "Plainpage - Chrome on Windows", so you can tell your browsers apart in Polar's customer portal.

No page content, browsing activity, email address, analytics or other personal data is included. As with any internet request, Polar's servers see your IP address. Requests go to api.polar.sh only.

Your purchase details (name, email, billing address, payment information) are collected by Polar when you buy and are handled under Polar's privacy policy. The extension never receives or stores your payment information.

If you do not use Plainpage Pro, the extension makes no network requests of its own.

Images and media inside an article are loaded by your browser from the website you are reading, exactly as they would be on the original page.

Limited Use

Plainpage's use of information received from Chrome APIs adheres to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Data is used only to provide the reader feature you see. It is not sold, not transferred to third parties except as described above for licence checks, not used for advertising, and not used to determine creditworthiness or for lending.

Deleting your data

  • Highlights and notes: Options → "Highlights and notes" → "Delete all".
  • Licence: Options → "Deactivate on this browser".
  • Everything: remove the extension from Chrome. All stored data is deleted with it.

Children

Plainpage is a general-purpose tool and does not knowingly collect data from anyone, including children.
